## 6. What Spain Did WELL — 3-Bullet Trend Summary

1. **Elite defensive record.** Unai Simón + back four (Porro, Cubarsí, Laporte, Cucurella) conceded **only 1 goal all tournament** — Simón's 7 clean sheets in 8 matches broke the all-time men's WC record (previously held since 1990 per ESPN). 519 consecutive minutes without conceding. The defensive spine won the title as much as any attacker.

2. **Midfield control under Rodri + Fabián Ruiz.** Luis de la Fuente benched Pedri (the Barcelona darling) for the more defensive, controlled Fabián Ruiz pairing with Rodri — a brave call that paid off. Rodri was the tournament's clear best player (Golden Ball), Sofascore-rated top of the pitch at halftime of the SF (7.2), and Spain won 57% of duels in the SF (Sofascore). Spain dominated possession in both SF and Final, suffocating France (Mbappé 6.1 Sofascore, 0.09 xG) and Argentina (Messi 3/10 ESPN, 10-man).

3. **Tournament redemption arc culminating in Ferran Torres.** Ferran Torres was "incessantly trolled" heading into the tournament (ESPN India), then came off the bench in the Final to score the 106' AET winner — the literal title-winning goal. Spain's depth (Torres, Pedri, Olmo/Baena, Oyarzabal all rotated) plus De la Fuente's willingness to drop big names (Pedri benched; Nico Williams protected through injury) meant the squad arrived fresh for the knockout push. The "Feeling of liberation" / "Destiny was written" narrative captured the media story of the tournament.
